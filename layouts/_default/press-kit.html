Biography

Pressgang Mutiny are Toronto’s “delightfully unhinged and uniquely talented” shantymen, dedicated to challenging conventional understanding of sea shanties from a place of deep immersion in their roots. They have travelled to discover where this work-song tradition came from, who sang it and why, and what it still has to say.

They are tradition-bearers who have worked to earn that responsibility by learning directly from those who came before them. In Barrouallie, St Vincent and the Grenadines, they learned from George “Tall 12” Frederick and filmed a documentary about him and the young people carrying his songs forward. They take their place as the next link in that chain seriously, and want their generation to have something of its own to contribute.

The often-erased contributions of Black sailors and Caribbean work-song traditions inform their creative work. Four-part harmony, new lyrics and arrangements, and collaborations with reggae and hip hop artists explore a shared musical heritage. Their third album, Departure (2026), features Carl Harvey of Toots and the Maytals and reggae MC Sunray Grennan, whose verses meet the band’s answering chorus on “Hill and Gully / Sally Rackett”. The band is back in the studio recording its next album.

On stage, traded leads and four-part harmony meet live instruments and DJ production, with audiences joining the responses. The band’s 2026 UK tour included Sidmouth, FolkEast, Whitby and Shrewsbury. Their workshops and community sings teach the harmonies and histories of the songs.

Pressgang Mutiny are Richard Kott, Josh Ludy, James McKie and Stefan Read.

Shows, Workshops & Community Sings

The concert brings together four-part harmony, live instruments and DJ production. Workshops give participants time to learn the vocal parts and the histories behind them. Community sings centre on call-and-response, with a way into the music for people who have never sung a shanty before.

Contact the band to discuss a concert, workshop or participatory sing for your programme.

Interviews & Features

The band’s work connects their time learning from George “Tall 12” Frederick in Barrouallie with the records they make alongside Toronto’s reggae and hip hop musicians. Their story and point of view explore what it means to inherit a living tradition, contribute to it and teach the next singers.
